Chapter I · Hario V60

THE 4:6 METHOD


🏆 TETSU KASUYA — 2016 WORLD BREWERS CUP CHAMPION (JAPAN)
☕ 20 g coffee💧 300 g water ⚖ 1 : 15🌡 93 °C⚙ medium-coarse
0:00Pour 60 g — the bloom. Let it breathe.
0:45Pour 60 g (scale reads 120 g). First 40% done — this half sets sweetness & acidity.
1:30Pour 60 g (180 g). Now the 60% — this half sets strength & body.
2:10Pour 60 g (240 g).
2:40Pour 60 g (300 g). Drawdown should finish ≈ 3:30.
The trick: grind coarser than you think. Each pour should fully drain before the next. → tuning chart overleaf!
src: honestcoffeeguide.com/brew-recipes/tetsu-kasuya-4-6-method · kurasu.kyoto (champion story)

Chapter I · Hario V60

THE ULTIMATE V60


🏆 JAMES HOFFMANN — 2007 WORLD BARISTA CHAMPION (UK)
☕ 30 g coffee💧 500 g water ⚖ 60 g/L🌡 100 °C, just off boil⚙ medium-fine☕☕ serves 2
0:00Bloom with 60 g (2× the dose) and give the brewer a good swirl.
0:45Pour 240 g steadily (scale reads 300 g) — aim to finish by ~1:15.
1:15Centre-pour 200 g more, slow and gentle (500 g total).
1:45Stir once each direction with a spoon to knock grounds off the wall, then swirl 2–3 times.
3:30Drawdown done — flat bed = even cup. Probably the most-watched pour-over recipe on the internet.
One kettle, no fuss, hard to get wrong. The swirls do the work that fancy pouring usually does.
src: honestcoffeeguide.com/brew-recipes/james-hoffmann-v60 · timer.coffee

Chapter I · Hario V60

THE FIVE-POUR


🏆 MATT WINTON — 2021 WORLD BREWERS CUP CHAMPION
☕ 20 g coffee💧 300 g water ⚖ 1 : 15🌡 93 °C → 88 °C⚙ medium
1.Five equal pours of 60 g. First pour at 93 °C — wet everything evenly.
2.Pour from a height — the agitation is the technique. No stirring, no swirling.
3.Wait ~30 s between pours, or until the bed runs dry / dripping nearly stops.
4.Later pours at 88 °C (he used two kettles!).
5.Final drawdown ~3:30. Total ≈ 6:50. Inhale before you sip.
Bitter & dry → grind coarser. Sour & thin → grind finer. The anti-4:6: height and splash instead of gentle spirals.
src: honestcoffeeguide.com/brew-recipes/matt-winton-v60-five-pour

THE ULTIMATE (DAILY) AEROPRESS


☕ JAMES HOFFMANN — THE EVERYDAY ONE
☕ 11 g coffee💧 200 g water 🌡 just off boil⚙ fine – med-fine🙂 upright
0:00No filter rinse needed. Add coffee, pour all 200 g at once.
0:15Insert the plunger just into the top — it stops the dripping.
2:00Steep done — hold brewer + plunger, swirl gently. No vortex! Just settle the crust.
2:30After 30 s rest, press slowly for ~30 seconds, all the way.
3:00Done. Cooler water for darker roasts.
The lazy-perfect morning cup — one pour, one swirl, one press. Our weekday default?

ICED AEROPRESS


🧊 JAMES HOFFMANN — FLASH-CHILLED
☕ 22 g coffee💧 240 g water @ 100 °C 🧊 160 g ice in the server⚙ finer end of medium🙂 upright
prepWeigh 160 g of ice into the vessel you'll press into. AeroPress on top.
0:00Pour all 240 g of hot water. Plunger in to hold the vacuum.
4:00Swirl the brewer, then rest 30 seconds.
4:30Press gently, straight onto the ice.
doneSwirl until the ice is gone, serve over fresh cubes.
Same trick as the flash brew: the ice is the missing brew water. Long hot steep + instant chill = big flavour, zero dilution guilt.

Chapter III · Chemex

HOFFMANN'S CHEMEX


🏆 JAMES HOFFMANN — 2007 WORLD BARISTA CHAMPION (UK)
☕ 30 g coffee💧 500 g water ⚖ 60 g/L🌡 100 °C⚙ medium☕☕ serves 2
0:00Bloom with 80 g, swirl. (3-layer side of filter faces the spout!)
0:45Pour steadily up to 300 g over ~30 s.
1:15Pour to 500 g over ~30 s.
1:45Stir once clockwise, once counter-clockwise. Then a gentle swirl.

Chapter IV · French Press

THE PATIENT PRESS


🏆 JAMES HOFFMANN — 2007 WORLD BARISTA CHAMPION (UK)
☕ 30 g coffee💧 500 g water 🌡 100 °C⚙ medium-coarse⏱ ~9:30 total
0:00Pour all 500 g of water in ~10 seconds. Walk away.
4:00Break the crust with a spoon — inhale, this is the best part.
4:30Skim off the floating grounds & foam with two spoons.
9:00After 5–8 more min of settling: rest the plunger just at the surface. Do NOT press down.
9:30Pour gently. Leave the last sip in the pot — that's where the silt lives.
The plunger becomes a sieve, not a piston. Cleanest french press you'll ever drink.
src: honestcoffeeguide.com/brew-recipes/james-hoffmann-french-press

THE CLASSIC 4-MINUTE


☕ STUMPTOWN COFFEE — HOUSE GUIDE
☕ 56 g coffee💧 850 g water ⚖ ~1 : 15🌡 ~96 °C (205 °F)⚙ coarse, like breadcrumbs

Chapter V · Moka Pot

ULTIMATE MOKA POT


🏆 JAMES HOFFMANN'S TECHNIQUE
⚙ AeroPress-fine (coarser than espresso)💧 just-boiled water🔥 medium-low heat
1.Fill the base with hot, just-boiled water — up to the valve for light roasts, a little less for dark roasts.
2.Fill the basket to the top with coffee. Level it. No tamping.
3.Screw together (use a towel — the base is hot!) and set on medium-low heat, lid open.
4.Watch: coffee should flow out slow and syrupy, like warm honey.
5.At the first gurgle/sputter — off the heat. (Cooling the base under the tap stops extraction dead.)
Hot-water start = less metallic bitterness, since the coffee never gets cooked while the pot warms up.
src: espressoinsiders.com — James Hoffmann's moka pot recipe explained

Chapter V · Moka Pot

MOKA ON ICE


🧊 BEAN POET — BREW-OVER-ICE TRICK
☕ basket full, medium-fine💧 base ~half full 🧊 ~6 cubes in the TOP chamber🔥 medium-low
1.Fill the basket to the top; base chamber ~halfway with water.
2.Medium-low heat, lid open, watch the chimney.
3.Just before coffee emerges, drop the ice into the upper chamber (sugar on the ice now, if you like it sweet).
4.Brew lands straight on the ice — instant chill, no watery dilution.
5.Serve over fresh cubes, splash of milk if that's your thing.
Iced moka for hot Manila afternoons — the concentrate stays punchy because it cools on contact.

Chapter VI · Cold Brew & Iced

OVERNIGHT CONCENTRATE


🏆 JAMES HOFFMANN'S METHOD
⚖ 1 : 10 (e.g. 100 g : 1 L)⚙ medium grind 🧊 fridge⏱ 12 h steep
1.Combine coffee + cold water at 1:10 in a jar. Stir to wet everything.
2.Lid on, into the refrigerator for 12 hours (14 h max — after that it just gets bitter).
3.Strain coarse first (sieve / cloth)…
4.…then pass through a paper filter for a clean, glossy concentrate.
5.Serve diluted 1:1 over ice. Keeps ~1 week chilled.
Perfect for the shop someday — batch it every night before closing, sell it every sunny Baguio afternoon.
src: calculate.coffee/cold-brew/james-hoffmann

ESPRESSO TONIC


🍋 THE CAFÉ SUMMER CLASSIC
☕ 1–2 shots espresso (or strong moka)🥤 tonic, well chilled ⚖ 1 : 3 espresso to tonic🧊 big clear cubes
1.Fill a tall glass with large ice cubes (small ones melt too fast).
2.Pour the tonic first — it keeps the bubbles alive and sets up the layers.
3.Pull the shots fresh.
4.Pour the espresso slowly over the back of a spoon so it floats on the tonic.
5.Garnish: strip of lemon, lime or orange peel — or a rosemary sprig. Stir at the table.
Bitter-sweet-fizzy and ridiculously photogenic — instant shop-menu material. Also works with cold brew concentrate instead of espresso.

Chapter VII · Kalita Wave

THE CHAMPION'S WAVE


🏆 JAMES McCARTHY — 2013 WORLD BREWERS CUP CHAMPION (USA)
☕ 24 g coffee💧 380 g water ⚖ ~1 : 16🌡 96 °C⚙ medium-coarse, fines sifted
0:00Pre-wet with 48 g — twice the dose. Let it bloom.
0:45Pour up to 190 g, keeping an even column of water above the grounds.
1:15Pour gently to 380 g — slow the flow right down (he used a flow-restricted kettle).
1:45Hands off. Let it draw down flat and even.
3:30Done — the bed should look like a level sandy beach.
He won the world title on this brewer — his edge was sifting out the fines before brewing. The flat bed extracts every particle the same.

Chapter VIII · Espresso

DIALLING IN — THE RECIPE


☕ THE "1:2 IN 30" RULE — every barista's starting point
☕ 18 g in💧 36 g out ⚖ 1 : 2⏱ 25–35 s🌡 ~93 °C⚙ fine as balance allows
1.Lock the dose: 18 g, weighed every time. Fill, distribute (WDT wiggle with a needle tool), tamp firm & level.
2.Pull to 36 g out — stop the shot on the scale, never by eye.
3.Check the clock: 25–35 s is the healthy window. Faster → grind finer. Slower → coarser.
4.Taste. Sour & sharp → finer grind, or stretch to 1:2.2. Bitter & harsh → coarser, or shorten toward 1:1.8.
5.Change one thing at a time, one step at a time. Log every shot →
Espresso is just three numbers: grams in, grams out, seconds. Everything else is romance.

Chapter VIII · Espresso

THE MILK BOARD

One double shot (~40 g out), five personalities — the milk is the costume:
macchiato40 g espresso + ~25 g milk — just “marked” with foam. Bold & tiny.
cortado40 g + ~55 g milk — near-equal partners, no foam theatrics. The balanced one.
flat white40 g + ~100 g milk, barely-there microfoam — silk with a strong coffee spine.
cappuccino40 g + ~100 g milk + ~1.5 cm foam cap — same bones as the flat white, airier hat.
latte40 g + ~230–240 g milk, ~1 cm foam — the mellow crowd-pleaser.
Going up the board = more milk, softer coffee. The shop menu writes itself from this one page.

Chapter IX · Siphon

CLASSIC SIPHON


☕ SPRUDGE — THE STANDARD METHOD
☕ 35 g coffee💧 560 g hot water (~96 °C) ⚖ 1 : 16⚙ medium, like beach sand⏱ ~3:30
prepFilter chain hooked in the top chamber. Fill the bottom globe with hot water, flame on.
riseVapor pressure pushes the water up. Once it's (almost) all up top — add the 35 g of coffee.
0:00Gentle stir to fully saturate the grounds.
0:30Second gentle stir of the crust. Then leave it be.
2:00Kill the heat — the brew rushes back down through the cloth. Drawdown ≈ 1:30, with the famous final burp.
Adjust the grind by taste, not by the clock. A domed, even bed of grounds up top = you did it right.

1615Coffee lands in Venice; the clergy grumble until Pope Clement VIII tastes it — and blesses it.
1650sLondon's 300+ coffeehouses become “penny universities” — a penny buys coffee & the best talk in town.
1600sThe Dutch plant Java; mixed with Yemen's beans it becomes the first blend: Mocha Java.
1723Officer de Clieu shares his ship's water ration with one seedling → Martinique → 18 million trees in 50 years.
1773The Boston Tea Party turns America into a coffee country for good.

BREW IT LIKE 1554 — THE CEZVE


🇹🇷 KURUKAHVECİ MEHMET EFENDİ — ISTANBUL HOUSE GUIDE
☕ 1 heaped tsp (~6 g) per cup💧 one demitasse of water per cup ⚙ extra-fine, like flour🍬 sugar optional — decide before brewing⏱ ~2 min
1.Measure the water with the serving cup, straight into the cezve.
2.Add coffee (and sugar, if taking). Stir well, then onto low heat.
3.Heat slowly. As the foam rises, lift off the heat and spoon a little foam into each cup.
4.Return for one more short rise, then pour gently down the side.
5.Serve with a glass of water — it wakes the mouth up for the coffee.
The foam is the pride of the cup — never walk away from a cezve. This is the drink the 1550s Istanbul coffeehouses were built on.

HOW TO TASTE LIKE A CUPPER

1.Smell first — the dry grounds, then the wet crust. Half of “taste” is aroma.
2.Slurp loudly. Really. Spraying coffee across the whole palate is the professional move.
3.Start at the centre of the wheel: is it fruity? nutty? roasted? Don't hunt for “blackberry” right away.
4.Move outward one ring: fruity → berry or citrus? nutty → almond or chocolate?
5.Note texture & finish: silky? syrupy? clean? lingering? Then write it down here. ✎
